Study area: Tirumala Hills, Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh

1. OBJECTIVE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
To investigate whether independently reported historical landslide events provide useful spatial context for the two provisional InSAR reference candidate regions.

5. INTERPRETATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The 2017 event coordinate is approximately 2.12 km from the nearest Region 1 candidate pixel and 1.43 km from the nearest Region 3 candidate pixel.
Its reported location accuracy is within 1 km. Both nearest-pixel distances exceed that reported accuracy range, when interpreted as a radius around the reported coordinate.
The 2013 event coordinate is approximately 2.93 km from Region 1 and 2.71 km from Region 3. Its reported location accuracy is within 5 km, so the uncertainty range encompasses both candidate regions.
Region 1 is farther from both reported event coordinates. This provides supporting historical spatial context for preferring Region 1 provisionally, but it does not establish ground stability.

6. LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. COOLR event coordinates are approximate and have reported location uncertainties.
2. A coordinate falling inside the AOI does not prove that the actual landslide footprint was entirely inside the AOI.
3. Distances are measured to candidate pixel centres, not to verified landslide boundaries or exact ground-failure footprints.
4. Historical event proximity cannot independently validate the physical stability of an InSAR reference region.
5. The historical events occurred in 2013 and 2017, whereas the available InSAR pairs cover August 2026.
6. Independent GNSS measurements, geological evidence and additional temporally consistent observations are still required.

7. CONCLUSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Region 1 remains the stronger provisional reference candidate based on the combined screening, terrain, GSI separation, sensitivity and historical-event distance investigations.
However, neither Region 1 nor Region 3 can currently be labelled confirmed stable ground. The COOLR investigation provides historical corroboration context only, not definitive validation.
